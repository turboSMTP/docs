Email authentication tells receiving mail servers that your messages genuinely originate from your domain — and that turboSMTP has your permission to send on your behalf. Without SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records in place, inbox providers treat your emails with far more suspicion, routing them to spam or rejecting them outright. Configuring these three records is free, takes only minutes, and can dramatically improve your deliverability and inbox placement. It also causes your own domain to appear in the email's `From` header rather than a turboSMTP-branded address, reinforcing subscriber trust.

<Warning>
  Only configure these DNS records manually if you are comfortable editing your domain's DNS settings. If you are unsure how to manage DNS records, ask your hosting provider for assistance. Mistakes in DNS can affect all email sent from your domain, not just mail routed through turboSMTP.
</Warning>

## SPF record

SPF (Sender Policy Framework) is a DNS TXT record that lists which mail servers are authorized to send email on behalf of your domain. When a receiving server gets your message, it checks whether turboSMTP's sending infrastructure appears in your SPF record — if it does not, the message may be rejected or flagged as suspicious.

Add the following TXT record to your domain's DNS:

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Type** | TXT |
| **Host** | `@` (root domain, e.g. `yourdomain.com`) |
| **Value** | `v=spf1 a mx include:spf.turbo-smtp.com ?all` |

```dns SPF Record
v=spf1 a mx include:spf.turbo-smtp.com ?all
```

The `include:spf.turbo-smtp.com` directive authorizes turboSMTP's sending servers. If you already have an SPF record on your domain, add `include:spf.turbo-smtp.com` to your existing record rather than creating a second TXT entry — domains must have only one SPF record.

## DKIM record

DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) adds a cryptographic signature to every outbound email. Receiving servers verify the signature against a public key you publish in DNS, confirming that the message body has not been tampered with in transit and that it truly originates from your domain.

Add the following TXT record to your domain's DNS under the `turbo-smtp._domainkey` subdomain:

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Type** | TXT |
| **Host** | `turbo-smtp._domainkey.yourdomain.com` |
| **Value** | `k=rsa; p=MIGfMA0GCSqGSIb3DQEBAQUAA4GNADCBiQKBgQDT3MWLni6so1q9eQggRYBCLHFjohZkCnYHH8gZNDBm6zRrodRVpWpJQW7x3cWWiuBhS1X0IfBB80l5tqFa+yc+mVgnk8tkUzOHFbPQPp4fi7egTpMtsQW/ZMrxw73SItNvPr72qvJTYZNPxarMx+ULjEWybcfEdXHPY8jslGcpCwIDAQAB` |

```dns DKIM Record
k=rsa; p=MIGfMA0GCSqGSIb3DQEBAQUAA4GNADCBiQKBgQDT3MWLni6so1q9eQggRYBCLHFjohZkCnYHH8gZNDBm6zRrodRVpWpJQW7x3cWWiuBhS1X0IfBB80l5tqFa+yc+mVgnk8tkUzOHFbPQPp4fi7egTpMtsQW/ZMrxw73SItNvPr72qvJTYZNPxarMx+ULjEWybcfEdXHPY8jslGcpCwIDAQAB
```

Some DNS providers require you to omit the root domain when entering the host — in that case use `turbo-smtp._domainkey` rather than `turbo-smtp._domainkey.yourdomain.com`. Check your provider's documentation if you are unsure.

## DMARC record

DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting & Conformance) builds on SPF and DKIM by telling receiving mail servers what to do when a message fails authentication checks. Even a permissive `p=none` policy signals to inbox providers that your domain is actively managed, improving trust signals and enabling you to receive aggregate failure reports.

Add the following TXT record to your domain's DNS:

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Type** | TXT |
| **Host** | `_dmarc.yourdomain.com` |
| **Value** | `v=DMARC1; p=none;` |

```dns DMARC Record
v=DMARC1; p=none;
```

The `p=none` policy puts DMARC in monitoring mode — failing messages are still delivered, but you gain visibility into authentication failures. Once you have confirmed that all legitimate email passes SPF and DKIM, you can tighten the policy to `p=quarantine` or `p=reject` for stronger protection.

## What if I can't edit my DNS?

If your hosting provider does not allow you to modify DNS records, you have two options:

- **Create a subdomain for sending.** Register a new domain or set up a subdomain (e.g. `mail.yourdomain.com`) with a provider that allows full DNS control, then use that domain as your sender address.
- **Contact your hosting provider.** Many providers will add DNS records on your behalf if you supply the exact values above.

Even if you cannot configure any DNS records, turboSMTP's **Re-enveloped Sender** fallback ensures your emails continue to be delivered. Messages will be sent using a turboSMTP-managed envelope address, and turboSMTP's own SPF and DKIM records will satisfy authentication checks on your behalf — though your custom domain will not appear as the authenticated sender.

## Benefits of configuring authentication

Setting up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC delivers tangible improvements to your email program:

- **Higher delivery rates** — authenticated mail bypasses many spam filters that would otherwise flag or reject unauthenticated messages.
- **Your domain in the From header** — your brand domain appears as the verified sender rather than a turboSMTP address, improving subscriber recognition and trust.
- **Better inbox placement** — inbox providers assign higher reputation scores to domains with consistent SPF and DKIM alignment.
- **Reduced spam folder placement** — authentication removes a major signal that spam filters use to divert bulk mail away from the inbox.
- **Protection against spoofing** — DMARC prevents bad actors from impersonating your domain in phishing attacks, safeguarding both your brand and your recipients.
